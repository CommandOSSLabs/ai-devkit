# The session map

Setup settles how to talk, not what the session is for. The first message that
carries pasted content — a live choice or not — opens on a map of the problem,
before any other line of the turn. Write it after grounding, so what it calls
known has been read.

The map is orientation, not the card. A reader should be able to say what is being
settled, what leaves at the end, and what nobody in the room can answer yet,
before they meet a fork. Measured, two first turns on the same paste opened on an
analogy and on `Today` — neither said what was being decided.

Headings and content in the companion language; the questions below are what each
line answers:

```
**What this session is about**
- **The problem:** <the feature and the question in front of it, in plain words, and why it came up now>
- **What we'll settle here:** <a shape to pick, a claim to check, a question to answer — and what leaves at the end>
- **What we have:** <what the paste states, what was read in the code and its history, the posture line>
- **What we don't have yet:** <each fact neither the paste nor the repo holds, one line each — who holds it, and whether the choice turns on it>
```

One or two lines a bullet. The map argues nothing: where the paste and the code
disagree it says so in a clause and leaves the correction to the card's
paste-vs-code heading; the pick belongs to the card, not here. A later paste gets
a new map only when it opens a problem this one did not cover.
